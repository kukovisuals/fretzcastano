Circuit Worlds

Take an automaton, tune its rules, and signals start to flow like current through wire — travelling, branching, and looping on a grid.

Signals as moving state

A signal is just a small pattern of live cells that the rules push along a track. The track constrains where the pattern can go; the rules decide how fast and in which direction it moves.

Gates from geometry

Where two tracks meet, the local neighbourhood determines whether a signal passes, blocks, or triggers a new one. Logic emerges purely from the shape of the wiring and the counting rule — no special-case code.

Keeping loops alive

A closed track can carry a signal forever if the rule set preserves it each lap. Get the timing right and you have memory — a value that persists by circulating rather than being stored in place.
