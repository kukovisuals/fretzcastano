0. The five things a pixel can be

Every pixel stores one small number. That number is a costume:

Receiver's perspective. A head never writes into the cell ahead of it — it can't; every pixel only writes itself. Movement happens because the empty cell ahead looks backwards, sees a head aimed at it, and dresses up as the next head. The old head undresses into trail. Two pixels acting alone = one thing appearing to move.

Mechanically: for side i, I read the neighbor opposite to travel-direction i — a right-mover can only arrive from my left, so "checking arrivals from direction right" means "reading my left neighbor".

the loop · decode & filter

Subtract the head offset — if what's left isn't a direction, skip

The neighbor's stored number, minus 2, should be a direction code 0–3. Empty gives −2, trail gives −1 — both fall outside the range and the loop moves on. This one subtraction is the whole "is it a head?" test:

exactly one claim · the wobble

10% of births swap to a random perpendicular direction

One more dice roll, over 0.9: instead of inheriting the claiming direction, pick one of the two directions on the other axis. Horizontal movers turn up or down; vertical movers turn left or right. Never a reverse:

Hidden quirk worth knowing: this dice roll is salted only by the pixel's position, never by the frame. So the 10% "turn tiles" are frozen into the grid — every wire that crosses the same pixel turns at the same spot, forever. The forks (3.2%) do use the frame, so they differ every pass.
